# Composite Camera Movement Reference

Use this reference to choose camera movements at the same conceptual level as Hitchcock's Dolly Zoom/Zolly: two or more single camera actions performed in synchrony.

## Core Move: Dolly Zoom / Zolly

Operation: physical dolly push or pull plus synchronized reverse zoom. The subject size stays nearly constant while the background stretches or compresses dramatically.

Use for: shock, realization, dread, emotional dislocation, sudden scale change, psychological pressure.

Short-video use: keep the subject locked in the center or stable third, cut on the background deformation, and motivate the move with a reveal or emotional turn.

## 1. Zoom Plus Physical Movement

### Push + Zoom In

- Operation: physically push forward while zooming in.
- Effect: subject enlarges quickly, background compresses and softens, pressure rises fast.
- Use for: shock, anger, confrontation, revealing a key object, suspense.

### Pull + Zoom Out

- Operation: physically pull back while zooming wider.
- Effect: subject shrinks, environment opens rapidly, loneliness or scale expands.
- Use for: endings, loss, separation, environment reveal, larger-world reveal.

### Diagonal Dolly Zoom

- Operation: 45-degree diagonal push or pull with reverse zoom.
- Effect: softer distortion than a straight Dolly Zoom, more mysterious than purely frightening.
- Use for: drama, relationship tension, emotional suspense, music-video atmosphere.

## 2. Orbit Plus Secondary Action

### Orbit + Crane

- Operation: circle the subject while slowly rising or descending.
- Effect: three-dimensional subject reveal, strong spatial hierarchy, ritual feeling.
- Use for: hero moments, product reveals, landmarks, weddings, atmospheric portrait shots.

### Back Orbit + Slow Pull

- Operation: rotate from front to back while pulling away.
- Effect: moves from emotion to environment, implying fate, isolation, or surrender.
- Use for: farewell, mystery ending, story turn, melancholy resolution.

### Orbit + Micro Push

- Operation: circle the subject while slowly tightening distance.
- Effect: tension accumulates without losing spatial context.
- Use for: pre-conflict moments, stare-downs, controlled escalation.

## 3. Passage Plus Push/Pan/Tilt

### Foreground Passage Push

- Operation: lock the subject and push through rails, branches, doorframes, wall gaps, fabric, or product foreground layers.
- Effect: foreground rushes past while subject stays readable; voyeuristic, immersive, deep.
- Use for: shop tours, street portraits, drama transitions, landscape passage, product reveals.

### Lateral Arc + Micro Pull

- Operation: move sideways on a smooth curved path while gently pulling back.
- Effect: more cinematic than flat tracking, with changing parallax and graceful release.
- Use for: walking narration, lifestyle moments, relationship staging.

### Dive Push + Tilt Down

- Operation: high camera or drone moves forward while tilting down from overhead into ground-level subject logic.
- Effect: macro world collapses into human/product scale.
- Use for: travel, sport, outdoor commercial, location reveal, opening shot.

## 4. Follow-Based Composites

### Follow + Push

- Operation: follow the moving subject while gradually pushing tighter.
- Effect: medium shot becomes close-up, emotion tightens.
- Use for: monologue, anxious walk, chase buildup, intimate lifestyle scene.

### Follow + Pull

- Operation: follow subject movement while pulling back.
- Effect: subject becomes smaller while environment grows.
- Use for: pressure from environment, freedom, loneliness, final walkaway.

### 45-Degree Follow + Micro Tilt

- Operation: follow from rear-side 45 degrees with slight up/down tilt adjustments.
- Effect: natural cinematic walking coverage, less flat than direct follow.
- Use for: everyday human movement, brand lifestyle, documentary-style narrative.

## 5. Rotation and Disorientation

### Roll + Dolly

- Operation: roll the camera horizon while pushing or pulling.
- Effect: spatial disorder, dream logic, mental collapse, time distortion.
- Use for: hallucination, crisis, extreme emotion, stylized transition.

### Dutch Tilt + Push

- Operation: tilt camera axis and slowly push forward.
- Effect: imbalance, unease, pressure, psychological threat.
- Use for: suspense, horror, conflict, deception, moral instability.

## 6. Transition Composites

### Whip Pan + Pull

- Operation: fast horizontal whip with slight pull during motion blur.
- Effect: seamless blur bridge between two clear moments.
- Use for: short video transitions, vlog, commercial montage, music edits.

### Pan + Dolly Out

- Operation: pan across environment or character relationship while pulling away near the end.
- Effect: natural contextual ending without a hard blackout.
- Use for: relationship geography, scene close, product-in-environment reveal.

## Selection Guide

- Atmosphere: use Orbit + Crane, Foreground Passage Push, or Diagonal Dolly Zoom.
- Human emotion: use Follow + Push, Follow + Pull, or Back Orbit + Slow Pull.
- Suspense/thriller: use Dolly Zoom/Zolly, Dutch Tilt + Push, or Roll + Dolly.
- Smooth transitions: use Whip Pan + Pull or Pan + Dolly Out.
- Product/commercial reveal: use Push + Zoom In, Orbit + Crane, Foreground Passage Push, or Dive Push + Tilt Down.

## Board Usage Rules

- Do not assign composite moves randomly. Tie every move to a story beat: reveal, pressure, intimacy, disorientation, release, or transition.
- Avoid using Dolly Zoom more than once in an 8-shot board unless the repetition has a mirrored story purpose.
- Pair intense moves with simpler shots before and after them so the rhythm breathes.
- Label each shot with both the operation and the intended audience effect.
