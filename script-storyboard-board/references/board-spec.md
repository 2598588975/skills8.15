# 16:9 Preproduction Board Specification

Use this reference to create a film production board, visual planning sheet, script storyboard, or commercial concept board.

## Canvas and Layout

- Aspect ratio: 16:9. Prefer 1920x1080, 2560x1440, or 3840x2160 for visual deliverables.
- Style: clean grid, restrained typography, cinematic but readable, professional director-board feeling.
- Structure: clear labeled sections, no decorative clutter, no repeated card-within-card layout.
- Visual rhythm: combine wide, medium, close, macro, overhead, and route-map views so the board does not feel like the same scene repeated.

Recommended layout:

1. Top shared creative direction bar.
2. Left or upper-middle character and style reference panel.
3. Right or middle environment and scene design panel with overhead path diagram.
4. Main storyboard strip or grid with about 8 numbered frames.
5. Bottom or side panels for lighting/mood, keywords, audio/tone, and cinematography notes.

## Shared Creative Direction Bar

Include:

- Shot count and runtime assumption.
- Unified palette with 3-5 colors.
- General location/environment premise.
- Texture and atmosphere rules.
- Continuity limits: identity locks, prop continuity, color accents, weather, and scene changes that are allowed.

Example fields:

- `Shots`: 8 shots, 45-60 seconds.
- `Palette`: storm green, basalt gray, warm signal amber, skin highlights.
- `Environment`: coastal cliff trail, wind, wet stone, low vegetation, distant water.
- `Constraint`: one protagonist, one route, no repeated composition, every shot changes scale or movement family.

## Character and Style Reference

Show or describe one model from multiple angles:

- Front view.
- Back view.
- Side view.
- Close-up identity anchor.
- Relaxed posture or candid pose.

Include costume/accessory references:

- Outerwear shape and fabric.
- Footwear, bag, jewelry, gloves, product, or practical prop.
- Hair, makeup, skin texture, weathering, and performance posture.

Identity consistency rules:

- Keep face shape, hair silhouette, body scale, core wardrobe, hero color accent, and signature prop consistent.
- Allow scene-specific variations only when motivated: wind, wetness, dust, unbuttoned collar, rolled sleeve, swapped lighting color, temporary handheld prop, or changed posture.

## Environment and Scene Design

Create one outdoor location with a dramatic natural feature, such as:

- Cliff edge, tidal rocks, canyon slot, lava field, alpine ridge, desert salt flat, storm lake, dense bamboo ravine, redwood grove, glacier wall, waterfall shelf.

Include an overhead schematic:

- Subject path with arrows.
- Start point, midpoint, reveal point, emotional turn, exit point.
- Camera positions labeled C1-C8.
- Shot type labels along the route: wide, medium, close, macro, overhead, POV, tracking, orbit, dolly zoom, transition.
- Hazards or natural anchors: edge, waterline, boulder, tree gap, doorway, ridge, bridge, fog bank.

## Storyboard Frame Schema

For each numbered frame, include:

- `Frame`: 01-08.
- `Shot size`: wide, medium, close-up, extreme close-up, macro, overhead, POV, insert.
- `Lens/camera feel`: wide distortion, portrait compression, long-lens isolation, macro texture, handheld intimacy, drone descent, crane rise.
- `Movement`: static, tracking, handheld, orbit, crane, push, pull, dolly zoom, whip pan, roll, foreground pass.
- `Action/emotion`: one clear beat.
- `Lighting/mood/style`: light quality, color, atmosphere, texture, and emotional temperature.
- `Continuity note`: what remains consistent and what changes.

Recommended 8-beat progression:

1. Establish location and route.
2. Introduce identity and object/brand/story hook.
3. Move through foreground layers.
4. First emotional or product reveal.
5. Composite movement escalation, often Dolly Zoom/Zolly or orbit-crane.
6. Intimate detail, macro, hand, face, object, or texture.
7. Spatial release or turn: pull back, overhead, or environment reveal.
8. Final image with altered emotional state and clean end-frame.

## Lighting, Mood, and Style Notes

Include visual examples or short descriptions for:

- Time-of-day transition: dawn, overcast morning, hard noon, golden hour, blue hour, night practicals.
- Light quality: rim light, bounce, reflected water light, storm diffusion, hard shadow, mist glow, sodium practicals.
- Texture: wet fabric, grain, dust, spray, lens bloom, skin pores, metallic edge, stone, foliage, product surface.
- Atmosphere: wind, fog, drizzle, heat shimmer, smoke, drifting particles, salt air.

## Mood and Keyword Block

Use compact tonal anchors:

- 5-9 keywords.
- 1 short theme sentence.
- 1 emotional arc sentence.

Examples:

- Keywords: isolation, pull of memory, wind pressure, controlled panic, tactile product detail, environmental scale.
- Theme: A small human decision feels huge against an indifferent landscape.
- Arc: calm observation shifts into distortion, then resolves into release.

## Audio and Tone

Include:

- Environment sounds: wind, footsteps, fabric movement, water, distant machinery, city echo, insects, room tone.
- Music style: restrained pulse, minimal piano, granular synth, percussive breath, orchestral swell, no-score silence.
- Sound design: whoosh, low-frequency rise, match cut hit, fabric snap, macro texture, heartbeat, reversed swell.
- Mix direction: close and dry, wide and reverberant, muffled subjective, crisp commercial polish.

## Cinematography Notes

Include the overall visual philosophy:

- Lens character: anamorphic softness, spherical clarity, wide-angle stretch, long-lens compression, macro texture.
- Movement grammar: controlled composite moves, motivated handheld, orbit for ritual, push for pressure, pull for release.
- Post feel: film grain, restrained contrast, halation, cool shadows with warm practicals, tactile texture, natural skin.
- Editing rhythm: establish, compress, distort, detail, release, resolve.

## Scene Variety Checklist

Before final output, verify:

- Adjacent frames do not share the same shot size and movement family.
- Camera height changes at least twice.
- Subject direction changes at least twice.
- Background depth changes from compressed to open or open to compressed.
- One frame uses foreground occlusion or passage.
- One frame uses macro or insert detail.
- One frame uses overhead or route-map logic.
- One frame functions as the hero motion beat.
